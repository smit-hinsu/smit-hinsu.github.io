Book review

Metaphors We Live By

★★★★★  ·  George Lakoff  ·  read March 2022

If a picture is worth a thousand words then a metaphor is worth a thousand pictures. - David Smith

Absolutely mind blowing!! This book gives a new way of understanding concepts and considerably changes how I feel, experience, understand art and rituals, think and communicate with others as well as myself.

Metaphors are considered as devices for poetic imagination and the rhetorical flourish. They aren't considered a necessary part of the ordinary language. However, as this book demonstrates, metaphors are pervasive and aren’t just limited to the literature. They also affect how we think, conceptualize and act based on that.

Example metaphors:

War metaphor for argument
“Your claims are indefensible.”
“He attacked every weak point in my argument.”
“I’ve never won an argument with him.”

Journey metaphor for love
“We’ll just have to go our separate ways.”
“We can’t turn back now.”
“where are we?”

Physical force metaphor for love
“There are no sparks”
“They are attracted to each other”

In the beginning of the book, the author develops a framework for different types of metaphors (structural, ontological and oriental) and related concepts like personification and metonymy. Then, it describes how metaphors are used for understanding, defining meaning, reasoning and acting based on it. Metaphors use sensory-motor domains based on the experience to draw inference in new domains.

The best part of the book is the discussion on myths of objectivism and subjectivism philosophies. Ancient philosophers Socrates and Plato as well as most modern western philosophers such as Hobbes considered figurative speech as an enemy of truth. They viewed poetry and rhetoric with suspicion as it stirs up emotions and blinds from the real truth. Personally, I feel poets use personal views and opinions instead of talking about them with an objective and balanced view. This leads to people consuming it without much deliberation and following those even in unrelated contexts. For example, Allama Iqbal’s pessimistic views around the future of Islam in the united India contributed to the partition and led to the misery of muslims during the partition as well as since then. However, it is not possible to objectively talk about everything. For example, experience, emotions, mind, aesthetic sensibilities, moral practices, etc. Also, poetry is an extremely effective tool of communication and allows internalization and grasping of new concepts easily. This book talks about an alternative myth of experientialism which combines objectivism and subjectivism by making use of the metaphoric concepts for subjectivity while understanding the metaphors objectively.

Key Takeaways:

- Depending on the choice of metaphors, it could highlight particular aspects of a concept while completely hiding the other aspects. For this reason, the choice of metaphor matters a lot and it could lead to completely different conclusions. For example, using “collaborative work of art” metaphor for love highlighting the active and collaborative nature of relationships could be more productive instead of using physical force metaphor i.e falling in love. Being aware of this could help coming up with better alternative metaphors.
- Human level AI inspired from the brain will need to be embodied as the experience plays a central role even in human abstract thinking.

The only criticism for the book is that it felt like a technical textbook with thorough analysis and careful reasoning making it a bit difficult to read. However, this actually made it easy to understand and follow the arguments. I would have also liked a lot more discussion on the implications and application of the ideas presented in the book instead of the predominantly theoretical discussion. Despite these, I absolutely loved the book as it was an eye-opener and transformative read.
