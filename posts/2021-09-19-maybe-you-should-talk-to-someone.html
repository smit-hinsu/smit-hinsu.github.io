Book review

Maybe You Should Talk to Someone

★★★★★  ·  Lori Gottlieb  ·  read September 2021

This was such a fascinating read that I wanted to get through it without stopping but at the same time it was so overwhelming and moving at times that I had to take breaks.

The book provides detailed information on what goes on in a therapy session with a psychotherapist and various psychology theories driving it. It is full of wisdom and insights on the human psyche, interpersonal relationships and the most difficult life events. In some of the therapy sessions, the way therapists make spontaneous observations to link simple client behaviors with their expressions, emotions and past was quite impressive.

What distinguishes this book from other non-fiction books written by experts in a field is that the author is a professional writer and was actually involved with Hollywood shows like Friends and ER. This helps the author present the content in an interesting nonlinear narration. It is full of foreshadowing and connects various dots over the time in unexpected ways. It is no surprise that the book is adapted for a TV show!

Key takeaways:

1) It could be tremendously useful to talk with someone when you are not feeling good for a period of time even though you think you know the root cause and have a solution. The author is a therapist and talking with a therapist helped her in a way that wouldn't have been possible otherwise.

2) Poor choices in early life or tragedies could be behind some of the highly obnoxious behaviors of people. It is possible to identify root causes of these with some curiosity, kindness and imagination and can be worked upon.

3) Around 10% of people seek therapy while there are more than 25% of people in the US on psychiatric drugs. People choose to go for quick solutions over therapy. Therapy helps better in most cases while the drugs are known to have significant side effects and don't address the root causes.
