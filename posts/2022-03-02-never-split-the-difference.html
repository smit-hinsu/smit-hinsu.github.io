Book review

Never Split the Difference

★★★★★  ·  Chris Voss  ·  read March 2022

“Life is negotiation: The majority of the interactions we have at work and at home are negotiations that boil down to the expression of a simple, animalistic urge: I want.”

This book is a masterpiece in communication. I had a misconception that communication is mainly about how to deliver a message effectively. However, this book shows that the major part of communication is about listening and making the other person feel heard. Once that is achieved, you both could reach the desired state.

The author was the FBI's chief international hostage and kidnapping negotiator and worked as a negotiator for 24 years. This book is a culmination of these real world experiences. In each chapter, the author talks about one hostage situation that his team had to deal with and the way they handle these situations is just mind boggling. The techniques and skills used in these situations are fundamental and also widely applicable in day to day situations. Despite the author’s great success at his job, it was good to see how the author was open minded and was always looking for ways to further sharpen these.

Any successful negotiation and communication involve the following:

- Active listening: Person who is speaking is revealing information and a trained listener can direct the conversation. Be patient. Be comfortable with silence and let the other person fill it.
- Empathy: Feeling and labeling the other person’s pain even when they don’t vocalize themselves or might not even be aware of that themselves.
- Safety & Autonomy: Allowing the other person to say “No” is useful for making it safe. “No” is better than a false “Yes” and a “No” may mean “Wait” or “I’m not comfortable with that.”.
- Being suggestive: Many communication guidelines discourage softening words and phrases “perhaps,” “maybe,” “I think,” and “it seems,”, but these are actually effective in taking out the aggression from the conversation.
- Open ended questioning: Open ended questions are useful in getting the other person to solve your issues. These questions start with “what”, “how” and sometimes “why” when it is not accusatory.
Be open to surprises and unknown unknowns (black swan) even after extensive preparation.

All these are very powerful and like anything powerful this has a huge negative side. Using these inappropriately is manipulation.

This was such an eye-opening read! The book also has nice summaries after each of the chapters and I will be referring to these periodically.

Other quotes I liked:

“He who has learned to disagree without being disagreeable has discovered the most valuable secret of negotiation.”

“That’s why negotiation is often called ‘the art of letting someone else have your way.’”

“Conflict brings out truth, creativity, and resolution.”

“The person across the table is never the problem. The unsolved issue is. So focus on the issue. This is one of the most basic tactics for avoiding emotional escalations. Our culture demonizes people in movies and politics, which creates the mentality that if we only got rid of the person then everything would be okay. But this dynamic is toxic to any negotiation.”
