Book review

The Science of Storytelling

★★★★★  ·  Will Storr  ·  read October 2021

Being a story junkie, I am always fascinated by the way some great authors and screenwriters create realistic and compelling characters that can be mapped to real people. For example, Mahabharata and Game of Thrones. So, I was eager to read the book and it didn't fail. I also believe that all forms of art have undiscovered scientific rationales behind them. This book uses neuroscience, psychology and evolution to shed some light on the art of story writing. The book is enlightening and has changed what I get out of reading novels and watching movies!

The author explains why, what and how of story writing using scientific literature and how that can be applied to various classic and famous stories. It explains what grabs readers’ attention to the story, what ignites their curiosity making it gripping, what takes them on an emotional rollercoaster and at the end transforms them forever.

It was interesting to see that some of the key elements required for a great story also make life interesting when applied to life. These are goal-directedness, understanding the core beliefs we hold and evolving them based on the experience.
Goal direction gives the story much of its tension and thrill. When faced with a crisis, we want the protagonist to react, plan, make decisions and reduce the chaos. It was found that the words like ‘do’, ‘want’ and ‘need’ appear twice as often in bestseller novels. This is similar to how the journey towards meaningful goals in life provides much of the happiness.

Good stories also require the protagonist to hold some strong belief because of early life experiences but it is flawed. For example, naive optimism, distrustfulness etc. This belief leads the protagonist to a difficult situation causing them to question who they really are. As they move away from the belief, it first actually makes the situation worse and brings their worst fears to the forefront. Despite that, they need to make the difficult choice if they want to evolve themselves at the climax to resolve the crisis. Similarly in life, we hold many beliefs and life requires understanding the self and evolving it by updating outdated beliefs even if that is very challenging. The book gives an example of Michael Corleone from The Godfather having the core belief that he is a law abiding citizen and doesn’t want to get involved in the mafia business. Later life experiences lead him to question this belief that is coming in his way in protecting the family.
